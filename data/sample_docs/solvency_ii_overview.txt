Solvency II overview (placeholder summary, not an official text)

Solvency II is the prudential regime that applies to insurers and reinsurers
operating in the UK and EU. It is built around three pillars.

Pillar 1 covers quantitative requirements. Insurers must hold enough capital
to survive extreme but plausible losses. Two thresholds matter here: the
Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR), which is the amount of capital needed to
withstand a 1-in-200-year shock over one year, and the Minimum Capital
Requirement (MCR), a lower threshold below which regulatory intervention
becomes automatic. Firms calculate the SCR either with a standard formula
set by the regulator or, subject to approval, with their own internal
model.

Pillar 2 covers governance and risk management. Firms must run an Own Risk
and Solvency Assessment (ORSA), which is their own forward-looking view of
the risks they face and the capital needed to cover them, distinct from the
Pillar 1 calculation.

Pillar 3 covers disclosure. Firms publish a Solvency and Financial Condition
Report (SFCR) annually, giving the market a public view of their solvency
position.
